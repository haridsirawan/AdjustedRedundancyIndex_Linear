Here are the basic definitions and concepts of graph theory as used in Kaveh’s *Structural Mechanics: Graph and Matrix Methods*.

### 1. Graph
A **graph** \(G = (V, E)\) consists of:
- a finite set \(V\) of **vertices** (or nodes),
- a set \(E\) of **edges**, each connecting a pair of vertices.

In structural mechanics, vertices usually represent **joints/nodes**, and edges represent **members/elements**.

### 2. Adjacency, Incidence, and Degree
- Two vertices are **adjacent** if an edge connects them.
- An edge is **incident** to its end vertices.
- The **degree** \(d(v)\) of a vertex is the number of edges incident to it.
- In a **directed graph**, edges have orientation; one distinguishes **in-degree** and **out-degree**.

### 3. Types of Graphs
- **Simple graph**: no loops and no multiple edges.
- **Multigraph**: allows multiple edges between the same pair of vertices.
- **Directed graph (digraph)**: edges have direction.
- **Undirected graph**: edges have no direction.

### 4. Subgraph
A **subgraph** is obtained by taking a subset of vertices and a subset of edges. A **spanning subgraph** contains all vertices of the original graph.

### 5. Walk, Path, and Cycle
- A **walk** is a sequence of alternating vertices and edges.
- A **path** is a walk with no repeated vertices.
- A **cycle** is a closed path whose only repeated vertex is the starting/ending vertex.
- A graph with no cycles is **acyclic**.

### 6. Connectivity
- A graph is **connected** if there is a path between every pair of vertices.
- A **component** is a maximal connected subgraph.
- A **cut-vertex** is a vertex whose removal disconnects the graph.
- A **bridge** is an edge whose removal disconnects the graph.

### 7. Tree and Spanning Tree
- A **tree** is a connected acyclic graph. A tree with \(n\) vertices has exactly \(n-1\) edges.
- A **spanning tree** is a tree that includes all vertices of the original graph.
- For a given spanning tree, every non-tree edge forms a unique **fundamental cycle**.
- Every tree edge defines a unique **fundamental cutset**.

### 8. Cutset
A **cutset** is a minimal set of edges whose removal disconnects the graph. A **fundamental cutset** is associated with a tree edge and consists of that edge plus some non-tree edges.

### 9. Matrix Representations
Important matrices used in structural mechanics include:
- **Incidence matrix** \(B\): rows correspond to vertices, columns to edges.
- **Adjacency matrix** \(A\): entries \(a_{ij}=1\) if vertices \(i\) and \(j\) are adjacent, otherwise \(0\).
- **Cycle matrix** \(C\): rows correspond to cycles, columns to edges.
- **Cutset matrix** \(S\): rows correspond to cutsets, columns to edges.

These matrices satisfy orthogonality relations such as  
\[
B C^{T} = 0
\]
for appropriately oriented incidence and cycle matrices.

### 10. Graph Vector Spaces
- The **edge space** is the vector space generated by the edges.
- The **cycle space** consists of all cycles and their linear combinations.
- The **cutset space** consists of all cutsets and their linear combinations.
- For a connected graph with \(V\) vertices and \(E\) edges:
  - dimension of cycle space \(= E - V + 1\),
  - dimension of cutset space \(= V - 1\).
- For \(c\) components:
  - cycle space dimension \(= E - V + c\),
  - cutset space dimension \(= V - c\).
- The cycle space and cutset space are orthogonal complements.

### 11. Planar and Dual Graphs
- A **planar graph** can be drawn in the plane without edge crossings.
- A **dual graph** has vertices corresponding to faces of the planar graph and edges corresponding to edges of the original graph.

### 12. Matroids
A **matroid** generalizes the idea of linear independence. In a **graphic matroid**, independent sets correspond to forests, and bases correspond to spanning trees. This concept is useful for structural independence analysis.

### 13. Relevance to Structural Mechanics
In Kaveh’s book, these graph-theoretic concepts are used to:
- represent skeletal structures as graphs,
- determine the **degree of kinematic indeterminacy (DKI)** and **degree of static indeterminacy (DSI)**,
- formulate the **matrix force method** and **matrix displacement method**,
- optimize bandwidth and profile of sparse structural matrices,
- analyse rigidity, independence, and decomposition of structures.

In short, graph theory provides the topological foundation for efficient matrix analysis of complex structural systems.
