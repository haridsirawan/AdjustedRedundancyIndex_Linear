
**Overview**

This book systematically presents how graph theory and matrix algebra can be used for the efficient computational analysis of large and complex skeletal structures. It transforms graph theory from an abstract mathematical subject into a practical tool for structural engineering, especially for handling sparse matrices and improving computational efficiency. The methods are applicable not only to skeletal structures but also to other networked systems such as hydraulic and electrical networks.

**Aims and Scope**

The main aim is to provide graph-theoretical and matrix-based computational tools for structural analysis, particularly for large-scale and complex framed structures. The book emphasizes sparse matrix formulation, topological properties, and efficient algorithms for structural mechanics.

**Main Contents**

1. **Graph Theory and Structural Topology (Chapters 1–3)**  
   - [[Basic definitions and concepts of graph theory.]]  
   - Vector spaces of graphs, including cycle and cutset spaces.  
   - Incidence and cycle matrices.  
   - Modelling skeletal structures as mathematical graphs.  
   - The union–intersection method for determining the degree of kinematic indeterminacy (DKI) and the degree of static indeterminacy (DSI).  
   - Rigidity of skeletal structures, complete matching, generic independence, and decomposition methods.

2. **Network Analysis and Matrix Methods (Chapters 4–6)**  
   - Structural analysis within a network-theory framework.  
   - Matrix displacement method.  
   - Matrix force method.  
   - Graph-theoretical force method, using suitable cycle bases to construct self-equilibrating force systems, making the force method computationally competitive with the displacement method.

3. **Computational Optimization and Advanced Topics (Chapters 7–10)**  
   - Ordering algorithms for bandwidth and profile optimization.  
   - Conditioning of structural matrices for numerical stability.  
   - Matroid theory and its application to independence analysis of skeletal structures.  
   - Configuration processing using graph-theoretical methods.

**Key Features**

- Includes many worked examples and exercises.  
- Provides computer programs in the appendix for practical implementation.  
- Strong emphasis on sparse structural matrices, which are essential for large-scale computation.  
- Balanced treatment of both force and displacement methods.  
- Highlights the author’s contributions to the graph-theoretical force method.

**Significance**

The second edition is a revised and expanded version of the 1992 first edition. It is regarded as a classic reference in computational structural mechanics for its integration of graph theory, matrix methods, and structural analysis. A third edition was published in 2004, but the 1995 second edition remains an important methodological text in the field.
